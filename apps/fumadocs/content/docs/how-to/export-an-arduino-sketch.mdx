**Show code** compiles your flow into an Arduino sketch. Download it, upload it with the Arduino IDE, and the board runs on its own — no computer, no USB tether, no Microflow.

## When you need it

- The project needs to run untethered, on its own power.
- You are handing the work to someone who writes Arduino code.
- You want to keep going in the Arduino IDE from here.
- You want to read what your flow actually does, in C++.

## Steps

1. **Set the board target first.** Open **Show code** and use the picker at the top. The sketch is generated for a specific chip, and the default is Arduino Uno. See [Choose a board target](/docs/how-to/choose-a-board-target).
2. **Read the top of the sketch.** Any problems Microflow found are listed there as comments, each naming the node it came from.
3. **Click *Download sketch*.** You get a `.ino` file named after your flow.
4. **Open it in the Arduino IDE.** Put it in a folder of the same name if the IDE asks — that is an Arduino convention, not a Microflow one.
5. **Tools → Board** and **Tools → Port**, then upload.
6. **Close Microflow.** The board keeps running.

## Verify it worked

Unplug the board from your computer and power it from a USB charger or a battery. If it still does what the flow did, the sketch is doing the work — nothing is left on your machine.

## What the icons next to the download mean

| Icon | Meaning | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nothing | The sketch generated cleanly. | Download it. |
| A warning triangle | The sketch generated, but part of your flow will not behave as the canvas suggests. | Read the comments at the top of the sketch. See [Warnings in the generated sketch](/docs/troubleshooting/sketch-warnings). |
| A cross | Generation failed. Download is disabled. | Fix the problem on the canvas — the message names the node. |

## What does not survive the export

The generated sketch runs on the board. Anything that depends on your computer does not:

- **[MQTT](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/mqtt) and the [Figma](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/figma) bridge** — on an ESP32 the sketch can carry network code; on an Uno or a Nano there is no network hardware at all.
- **[LLM](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/llm)** — the model runs on your machine.
- **[Hotkey](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/hotkey) and [MIDI](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/midi)** — your keyboard and your MIDI controller are attached to your computer.
- **[Monitor](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/monitor)** — there is no canvas to draw on.

A flow built around those nodes is a flow that wants to stay connected. That is a legitimate choice — plenty of installations run with a laptop in the cupboard.
