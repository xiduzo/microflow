**Show circuit** draws the wiring your flow implies — every component you have used, with the pin each one is set to. It is generated from the flow each time you open it, so it cannot fall out of date with the canvas.

## When you need it

- You have built a flow and now need to wire the physical side.
- You wired something from a kit's own diagram and it does not work.
- You loaded a template and need to know what it expects.
- You are handing a project to someone else to build.

## Steps

1. Open **Show circuit** in the sidebar, under the current flow.
2. Read the diagram once, whole, before touching a wire.
3. Wire one connection at a time. Check the pin against the diagram *before* pushing the wire in, not after.
4. Wire power and ground last, so nothing is live while you work.

## What the diagram is telling you

- **Every component on the canvas appears**, with the pin from its settings.
- **Change a pin on the canvas and the diagram changes** the next time you open it. The canvas is the source of truth.
- **Components that need power and ground show both.** Those are the connections people skip and then spend an hour on.

## What it does not tell you

The diagram is generated from your flow, so it only knows what your flow knows.

- **It does not know your components' quirks.** Whether your relay module is active-low, whether your RGB LED is common-anode, whether your particular sensor breakout has a pull-up on board — none of that is in the flow.
- **It does not size resistors for you.** An LED needs a current-limiting resistor; 220 Ω is a sensible default with a 5 V board.
- **It does not know about power budget.** A motor, a relay coil, or an LED strip will draw more than a USB port can supply. Power them separately and join the grounds.

## Verify it worked

Wire it, then drive one output at a time from a [Constant](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/generate/constant) node. If the component responds, that leg of the wiring is right. Doing this one component at a time is much faster than building the whole thing and debugging it as a unit.

## If the wiring is right and nothing happens

See [Nothing happens on the board](/docs/troubleshooting/pin-conflicts). The pin number in the node's settings not matching the pin on the board is the single most common cause, and the circuit view is the fastest way to catch it.
