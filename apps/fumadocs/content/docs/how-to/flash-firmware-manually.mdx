---
title: Flash firmware manually
description: Take over the firmware step yourself, when Studio's automatic flash will not do it.
---

Studio handles firmware on its own. Every time it connects to a board it probes for Microflow's firmware, flashes it if it is missing, and connects. A board that has been used for something else — a sketch from the Arduino IDE, another tool, a colleague's project — recovers on the next connection with no action from you.

So doing it by hand is rare. It is worth doing when:

- The status cycles between **Connecting** and **Flashing firmware** and never settles.
- The board connects but no pin ever responds, on any flow.
- You interrupted a flash by unplugging the board mid-way.
- You want to keep working while the board holds a sketch of your own.

## Before you start

Rule out the two causes that make an automatic flash fail, because they will make a manual one fail too:

1. **The cable.** A charge-only cable connects nothing. See [Board not detected](/docs/troubleshooting/board-not-detected#1-the-cable).
2. **Another program holding the port.** The Arduino IDE's Serial Monitor is the usual one. Close it — including the IDE itself on Windows.

## Let Studio do it

The reliable path is to let the automatic flow run cleanly:

1. Close every other program that talks to the board.
2. Unplug the board. Count to five.
3. Plug it back in, directly into the computer rather than a hub.
4. Watch the sidebar. **Connecting**, then **Flashing firmware**, then a port name.
5. **Do not unplug during Flashing firmware.** It can take up to a minute.

That is the whole procedure for the great majority of cases.

## Flash from the Arduino IDE

If the board will not take a flash from Studio at all, prove the board can be written to at all:

1. Open the Arduino IDE.
2. **Tools → Board** — pick your board. **Tools → Port** — pick the port.
3. **File → Examples → 01.Basics → BareMinimum**, and upload it.

- **The upload succeeds** — the board and the cable are fine. Close the IDE completely and let Studio connect again.
- **The upload fails**, particularly with `avrdude: stk500_recv()` — the board has a bootloader problem. That needs hardware-level recovery (an ISP programmer) and is outside what Microflow can do.

## Verify it worked

Add any [Sense](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense) node and a [Monitor](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/monitor), connect them, and interact with the component. Values appearing means firmware, board, and flow are all healthy.

If the Monitor stays blank, the firmware is not the problem — see [Nothing happens on the board](/docs/troubleshooting/pin-conflicts).
