---
title: Flash firmware manually
description: Take over the firmware step when Studio's automatic flash does not complete.
---

Studio handles firmware on its own. On every connection it probes the board, flashes the firmware if the probe fails, and connects. A board you have used for something else recovers on the next connection.

<Clip title="A first-time firmware flash" duration="0:30" spec="firmware-flash">
The status moves through Connecting and Flashing firmware to a port name, unedited and at real speed.
</Clip>

Doing it yourself is therefore rare. Do it when:

- The status moves between **Connecting** and **Flashing firmware** and never settles.
- The board connects and no pin responds, on any flow.
- You unplugged the board during a flash.
- You want to keep a sketch of your own on the board while you work.

## Before you start

Rule out the two causes that break an automatic flash and a manual one alike.

1. **The cable.** A charge-only cable connects nothing. See [Board not detected](/docs/troubleshooting/board-not-detected#1-the-cable).
2. **Another program holding the port.** The Arduino IDE's Serial Monitor is the usual one. Close it, and on Windows close the whole IDE.

## Let Studio run the flash cleanly

1. Close every other program that talks to the board.
2. Unplug the board and wait five seconds.
3. Plug it into the computer directly, not into a hub.
4. Watch the sidebar: **Connecting**, then **Flashing firmware**, then a port name.

Do not unplug during **Flashing firmware**. It takes up to a minute.

This procedure fixes most cases.

## Flash from the Arduino IDE

If Studio cannot flash the board at all, check that anything can write to it.

1. Open the Arduino IDE.
2. Select **Tools → Board**, then **Tools → Port**.
3. Open **File → Examples → 01.Basics → BareMinimum** and upload it.

**If the upload succeeds**, the board and the cable work. Close the IDE completely and let Studio connect.

**If the upload fails**, and especially if it reports `avrdude: stk500_recv()`, the board has a bootloader fault. Recovery needs an ISP programmer and is outside what Microflow can do.

## Verify it worked

1. Add any [Sense](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense) node.
2. Add a [Monitor](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/monitor) node and connect the two.
3. Touch the component.

Values in the Monitor mean the firmware, the board, and the flow all work. A blank Monitor means the firmware is not the problem — see [Nothing happens on the board](/docs/troubleshooting/pin-conflicts).
