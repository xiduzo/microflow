---
title: Choose a board target
description: Tell Microflow which board a flow is for, so the diagram and the sketch are right.
---

Every flow carries a **board target**: the board its generated sketch and wiring diagram are built for. The default is Arduino Uno.

## When to change it

- You use a Nano or an ESP32.
- The circuit view shows pins your board does not have.
- The generated sketch does not compile in the Arduino IDE.
- You get warnings about pins that exist on your board.

## Steps

1. Open **Show code** in the sidebar, under the current flow.
2. Use the board picker at the top of the view.
3. Select the board on your desk.

Studio regenerates the sketch, its warnings, and the circuit view.

## The targets

| Target | Pins | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arduino Uno** | Digital 0–13, analog A0–A5. PWM on 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11. | The default. |
| **Arduino Nano** | The same as the Uno. | Interchangeable with the Uno. |
| **ESP32** | Different numbering, more pins. | The only target with networking. Never use GPIO 6 to 11: they drive the chip's flash, and the board does not boot without them. |

## What the target changes

The target changes three things:

- The generated Arduino sketch.
- The wiring diagram in the circuit view.
- The validation behind the [sketch warnings](/docs/troubleshooting/sketch-warnings): which pins exist, which support PWM, how many analog inputs there are, and whether the board has networking.

The target does not change how a flow runs over USB from Studio. That works on the connected board whatever the target says.

That is why a wrong target goes unnoticed. Everything works on the desk, and the exported sketch does not.

## Verify it worked

Open **Show code** and read the warnings at the top. Warnings about pins that exist on your board, or about missing PWM on a pin you know supports it, mean the target is still wrong.
