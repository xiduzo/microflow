---
title: Choose a board target
description: Tell Microflow which board a flow is for, so the diagram and the sketch are right.
---

A flow carries a **board target** — the board its generated sketch and wiring diagram are built for. It defaults to **Arduino Uno**. If you are using something else, change it, or the two most useful views in Studio will quietly describe the wrong hardware.

## When you need it

- You are using a Nano or an ESP32.
- The circuit view shows pins that are not on your board.
- The generated sketch will not compile in the Arduino IDE.
- You are getting warnings about pins that plainly exist.

## Steps

1. Open **Show code** in the sidebar, under the current flow.
2. Use the board picker at the top of the view.
3. Pick the board that is actually on your desk.
4. The sketch, its warnings, and the circuit view all regenerate.

## The targets

| Target | Pins | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arduino Uno** | Digital 0–13, analog A0–A5. PWM on 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11. | The default. |
| **Arduino Nano** | Same chip and pin map as the Uno, smaller board. | Interchangeable with the Uno as far as a flow is concerned. |
| **ESP32** | Different numbering, more pins, fewer of them safe. | The only target with networking. **Avoid GPIO 6–11** — they drive the chip's flash and using them stops the board booting. |

## What the target does and does not affect

**It affects:**

- The generated Arduino sketch.
- The wiring diagram in the circuit view.
- The validation that produces [sketch warnings](/docs/troubleshooting/sketch-warnings) — which pins exist, which do PWM, how many analog inputs there are, and whether networking is available.

**It does not affect** running a flow over USB from Studio. That works on the connected board whatever the target says. Which is exactly why a wrong target goes unnoticed: everything works on the desk, and then the exported sketch does not.

## Verify it worked

Open **Show code** and check the warnings at the top. Warnings about pins that do not exist, or about missing PWM on a pin you know is fine, usually mean the target is wrong rather than the flow.
