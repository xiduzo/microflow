Several people can have the same flow open and see each other's changes as they happen, like a Figma file. Access is granted per person, by email — the link on its own grants nothing.

## What you need

- A Microflow account, and a saved flow. The **Local Flow** cannot be shared — it lives in your browser.
- An email address for each person you are inviting.

## Steps

1. Open the flow, then open its **settings** from the dock at the bottom of the canvas.
2. Find the **Collaborators** section and click **Share**.
3. Type their email address.
4. Choose a role:
   - **Editor** — can change the flow.
   - **Viewer** — can open, pan, zoom, and select, but change nothing.
5. Submit. If they already have an account they are added immediately; if not, the row shows **Invited** and they get access when they sign up.
6. Send them the link from the same dialog.

<Callout type="warn" title="The link does not grant access">
Copying the link and sending it to someone who has not been added does nothing for them. The link opens the flow for people who already have access — the email invite is what grants it.
</Callout>

## Verify it worked

Their row appears in the collaborators table with their role. Once they open the flow, their cursor appears on your canvas with their name on it.

## Change or remove access

Same page. Change someone's role from the row's menu, and it takes effect on their screen immediately — a viewer gets a **View only** badge, their nodes stop moving, and the delete key stops doing anything.

A pending invite can be withdrawn before it is accepted. Only the flow's owner can invite, change roles, or delete the flow.

## Who runs the hardware?

The flow is shared. The hardware is not.

The board is plugged into one person's machine, so everybody else's sidebar correctly says no microcontroller is connected. The **values** from that board travel with the flow, so a collaborator with no hardware at all can watch the sensors and edit the flow that reacts to them — which is how you run a workshop with one kit. See [Collaborate without hardware](/docs/how-to/collaborate-without-hardware).

## What stays in sync

**Synced:** node positions, node settings, edges, and the live values flowing through the flow.

**Per machine:** which board is connected, and the local view — your zoom and pan are yours.

## Conflicts

Microflow uses [Yjs](https://yjs.dev), so simultaneous edits merge rather than erroring. If two of you change the same setting at the same instant, one of them wins. There is no lock, no merge dialog, and no way to lose the document — but there is also nothing to stop you quietly undoing each other, so talk first.

Edits made offline are stored locally and merged when you reconnect. There is no save button.
