## Set up before you record

- Board already connected, sidebar showing the port name. Connecting is the previous video's job.
- Empty canvas, zoom at 100 %.
- Do **not** load the Blink template. This video builds it by hand; the template gets a mention at the end.

## Script

### 0:00 — The promise

**Screen:** Close-up of the Arduino, LED beside pin 13 dark.

**Narration:**
> "There is a small LED soldered to your board, next to pin thirteen. By the end of this video it is blinking, and you have built the flow that drives it."

### 0:15 — Add the Interval node

**Screen:** Empty canvas. Press `Cmd/Ctrl + K`. The **Add node** palette opens, grouped by category. Type `interval`. The Interval entry filters to the top under **Generate**. Press Enter. The node lands on the canvas.

**Narration:**
> "Every flow starts with a node that produces something. Press Command K, or Control K on Windows, to open the node palette. It is searchable, so type what you want. Interval emits a tick on a schedule. Enter places it."

**Screen:** Click the node to open its settings. Set the interval to `1000`.

**Narration:**
> "One thousand milliseconds. That is one tick per second."

**On-screen text:** `Cmd/Ctrl + K — add a node`

### 0:55 — Add the LED node

**Screen:** `Cmd/Ctrl + K` again, type `led`, and pause for one second on the results so the viewer sees several matches — LED, LED Matrix, LED Strip. Select **LED** under **Express**. Place it to the right of Interval.

**Narration:**
> "Now a node that acts on the tick. Search for LED. There are three: a single LED, a matrix, and a strip. Take the plain one, under Express. Express nodes act on the physical world."

**Screen:** Open its settings and set **Pin** to `13`.

**Narration:**
> "Pin thirteen, because that is the LED already on the board. No wiring today."

### 1:35 — Connect them

**Screen:** Zoom to 150 %. Hover over the Interval node's right edge until the handles are visible. Drag from the `event` handle across to the `toggle` handle on the left of the LED node. Hold on the completed edge for two seconds.

**Narration:**
> "The handles on the right are outputs. The handles on the left are inputs. Drag from the Interval's event output to the LED's toggle input. That line is an edge, and it carries the tick to the light."

**On-screen text:** `event → toggle`

### 2:00 — It blinks

**Screen:** Cut to the hardware close-up. The on-board LED blinks once a second. Hold for five seconds — long enough to count three blinks.

**Narration:**
> "No compile step, no run button. The flow started when the edge connected."

### 2:20 — Deliberate failure: the wrong pin

**Screen:** Back on the canvas. Open the LED settings and change **Pin** from 13 to 12. Cut to the hardware: dark. Hold for four seconds. Cut back and set it to 13 again. Cut to the hardware: blinking.

**Narration:**
> "Here is the mistake everyone makes once. Change the pin to twelve and the light stops. Nothing on screen reports a problem, because there is none: Studio is switching pin twelve, and nothing is attached to pin twelve. When a flow looks right and the hardware does nothing, check the pin number first."

**On-screen text:** `[pin 12 — flow still running, nothing wired to it]`

### 3:00 — What just happened

**Screen:** Zoom to fit (`Shift + 1`) so both nodes and the edge fill the frame.

**Narration:**
> "Two nodes and one edge. One node generates a value, the value travels along the edge, another node expresses it in the physical world. Every flow repeats that shape. It also ships as the Blink template, under Templates."

## Deliberate failure

The pin-12 change at 2:20. It must be a real edit with the real (absent) result on camera.

## Post-production notes

- Zoom to 150 % for every handle drag. At 100 % the handles are too small to follow.
- The hardware shot needs a fixed camera. Do not re-frame between the working and non-working takes — the viewer should be able to tell nothing changed except the pin.
- Caption the palette search string as `[typing: interval]`.
