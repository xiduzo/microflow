## Set up before you record

Build all four demos physically and get them working *before* the camera is on. Nothing is assembled on screen.

1. **Blink** — Arduino Uno, on-board LED on pin 13, Interval → LED.
2. **Knob and lamp** — potentiometer on A0, LED on pin 9 through a 220 Ω resistor, Potentiometer → Map → LED.
3. **Motion and sound** — PIR on pin 7, piezo on pin 8, Motion → Delay → Piezo.
4. **Sensor into Figma** — LDR on A0 driving a Figma variable through the Hardware Bridge, with a prototype whose background lightens as the room does.

Each demo gets its own saved flow so you can switch between them in the flow switcher without building anything.

## Script

### 0:00 — Cold open

**Screen:** Overhead shot of a breadboard. A hand turns a potentiometer; an LED fades up and down in time with it. No app visible. Five seconds of this before anyone speaks.

**Narration:**
> "This is a knob turning a light on and off. Nothing surprising — except that nobody wrote any code to make it happen."

### 0:20 — The reveal

**Screen:** Pull back or cut to a screen recording of Microflow Studio with the knob-and-lamp flow on the canvas: three nodes, two edges. As the hand turns the knob off-screen, the values on the nodes update live.

**Narration:**
> "This is the whole program. A node that reads the knob. A node that rescales the reading. A node that drives the light. You connect them by dragging a line between them, and it runs the moment you plug your board in."

**On-screen:** Zoom to 150 % on the value readout of the Potentiometer node so the changing number is legible.

### 0:50 — Three more things people build

**Screen:** Montage, roughly 15 seconds each, cutting between the hardware and the canvas that drives it.

1. Blink: the on-board LED pulsing, canvas showing Interval → LED.
2. Motion and sound: a hand waving past a PIR sensor, a piezo chirping a beat later, canvas showing Motion → Delay → Piezo.
3. Figma: a hand covering a light sensor, and a Figma prototype on a second monitor dimming in response.

**Narration:**
> "A light that blinks on a timer. A sensor that notices someone walk past and sets off a buzzer. A prototype in Figma that reacts to the actual light level in the room — because the sensor on the desk is feeding it."

### 1:40 — Who it is for

**Screen:** Wide shot of the desk: laptop, breadboard, a small pile of components.

**Narration:**
> "Microflow is for people who want to build something physical without learning to program a microcontroller first. If you can plug in a USB cable and drag a box across a screen, you have everything you need. It is free, it is open source, and it runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux — or straight in a Chrome browser if you would rather not install anything."

### 2:05 — Where to start

**Screen:** Studio, empty canvas, sidebar visible. Cursor rests on the flow switcher.

**Narration:**
> "The first tutorial takes about five minutes and needs nothing except a board and a USB cable. It is linked right below this video."

**On-screen text (final card, 4 seconds):** `Your first flow — docs.microflow.tech/docs/learn/first-flow`

## Deliberate failure

None. This is the only video in the series without one — a first impression is not the place to show something breaking.

## Post-production notes

- Keep the cold open silent apart from room tone. The first spoken word lands at 0:05.
- The montage cuts must be on the beat of the physical event, not the narration.
- No music under the narration. A short music sting on the final card only.
