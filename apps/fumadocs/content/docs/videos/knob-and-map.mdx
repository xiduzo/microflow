## Set up before you record

- LED on **pin 9**. It must be a PWM pin — the LED node's brightness input refuses to connect on a pin without one, and that refusal is part of the script.
- Potentiometer wired: outer legs to 5 V and GND, wiper to A0.
- Studio open, board connected, canvas empty.

## Script

### 0:00 — Why this one is different

**Screen:** Hardware: a knob and a dark LED.

**Narration:**
> "Up to now everything has been on or off. A knob is not on or off — it is a number, anywhere between nothing and all the way round. Getting that number to mean something to an LED is what this video is about."

### 0:25 — Wire the potentiometer

**Screen:** Overhead. Three wires, one at a time.

**Narration:**
> "A potentiometer has three legs. The outer two go to five volts and ground — it does not matter which way round, it only flips which direction counts as up. The middle leg is the one that reports the position, and it goes to A-zero, an analog input."

**On-screen text:** `outer legs → 5V / GND · wiper → A0`

### 1:00 — Read it on screen

**Screen:** `Cmd/Ctrl + K`, add **Potentiometer**, set **Pin** to `A0`. Zoom to 150 % on the node. Turn the knob slowly through its full range while the value on the node updates.

**Narration:**
> "Add a Potentiometer node, set its pin to A-zero, and turn the knob. The number on the node is live. All the way one way, zero. All the way the other, one thousand and twenty-three. That range is not arbitrary — it is what the board's analog-to-digital converter gives you, and every analog sensor you plug in will speak in it."

**On-screen text:** `0 – 1023`

### 1:40 — Deliberate failure: straight to the LED

**Screen:** Add an **LED** node, set **Pin** to `9`. Drag from the Potentiometer's `value` output to the LED's `value` (brightness) input. Cut to hardware and turn the knob: the LED jumps to full brightness almost immediately and stays there through most of the knob's travel.

**Narration:**
> "Wire the knob straight into the LED's brightness input and it half works, which is worse than not working. Brightness runs zero to two hundred and fifty-five. The knob goes up to a thousand and twenty-three. So a quarter of a turn takes you to full brightness and the rest of the knob does nothing at all. The two ends are speaking different languages."

**On-screen text:** `[knob 0–1023 → brightness 0–255]`

### 2:20 — Add the Map node

**Screen:** Delete the edge. `Cmd/Ctrl + K`, search `map`, place the **Map** node between the two. Open its settings: **From** `0`–`1023`, **To** `0`–`255`. Connect Potentiometer `value` → Map `value`, and Map `to` → LED `value`.

**Narration:**
> "The Map node is the translator. Tell it what range comes in — zero to one thousand and twenty-three — and what range should come out — zero to two hundred and fifty-five. It scales everything in between, proportionally. Knob into Map, Map's *to* output into the LED's brightness."

**On-screen text:** `from 0–1023 → to 0–255`

### 3:10 — Fade it

**Screen:** Hardware close-up. Turn the knob slowly across its whole travel, both directions. The LED tracks it evenly. Hold for six seconds.

**Narration:**
> "Now the whole knob does something, and it does it evenly."

### 3:35 — Why the pin has a tilde

**Screen:** Back on the canvas. Open the LED node's settings and change **Pin** to `7`. The brightness input becomes unconnectable and the node's hint reads `requires a ~ pin`. Change it back to 9.

**Narration:**
> "One thing that will catch you out. Brightness only works on pins marked with a tilde on the board — those are the ones that can fake an in-between voltage by switching very fast. Move the LED to pin seven and Studio will not let you connect brightness at all; it tells you it needs a tilde pin. On an Uno those are pins three, five, six, nine, ten and eleven."

**On-screen text:** `PWM pins on an Uno: 3 · 5 · 6 · 9 · 10 · 11`

### 4:05 — Where else Map earns its keep

**Screen:** Canvas with the working flow, cursor idle.

**Narration:**
> "Any time two things disagree about their range, Map sits between them. A knob driving a servo — nought to a thousand and twenty-three into nought to a hundred and eighty degrees. A light sensor driving a volume. A distance sensor driving a colour. Same node, different numbers."

## Deliberate failure

The unmapped connection at 1:40. Record the real behaviour — the LED reaching full brightness early and flattening out — not a simulation of it.

## Post-production notes

- The knob turn must be slow enough to read the value on screen. Rehearse it.
- Split-screen the canvas and the hardware for the 3:10 fade so the number and the light are visible together.
- Caption the hint string exactly: `[hint: requires a ~ pin]`.
