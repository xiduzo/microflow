## Set up before you record

- LED on **pin 9**. It must be a PWM pin: the LED node refuses the brightness connection without one, and that refusal is in the script.
- Potentiometer wired: outer legs to 5 V and GND, wiper to A0.
- Empty canvas, board connected.

## Script

### 0:00 — Why this one is different

**Screen:** A knob and a dark LED.

**Narration:**
> "Up to now everything has been on or off. A knob is neither. It is a number, anywhere between nothing and all the way round. Getting that number to mean something to an LED is what this video is about."

### 0:25 — Wire the potentiometer

**Screen:** Overhead. Three wires, one at a time.

**Narration:**
> "A potentiometer has three legs. The outer two go to five volts and ground. Swapping them only flips which direction counts as up. The middle leg reports the position, and it goes to A-zero, an analog input."

**On-screen text:** `outer legs → 5V / GND · wiper → A0`

### 1:00 — Read it on screen

**Screen:** Add a **Potentiometer** node, set **Pin** to `A0`, zoom to 150 %. Turn the knob slowly through its full range.

**Narration:**
> "Add a Potentiometer node, set its pin to A-zero, and turn the knob. All the way one way, zero. All the way the other, one thousand and twenty-three. That range is what the board's analog-to-digital converter gives you, and every analog sensor speaks in it."

**On-screen text:** `0 – 1023`

### 1:40 — Failure: straight to the LED

**Screen:** Add an **LED** node on pin `9`. Connect Potentiometer `value` to LED `value`. Cut to the hardware and turn the knob. The LED reaches full brightness early and stays there.

**Narration:**
> "Wire the knob straight into the brightness input and it half works, which is worse than not working. Brightness runs zero to two hundred and fifty-five. The knob goes to a thousand and twenty-three. A quarter turn takes you to full brightness, and the rest of the knob does nothing."

**On-screen text:** `[knob 0–1023 → brightness 0–255]`

### 2:20 — Add the Map node

**Screen:** Delete the edge. Add a **Map** node between the two. Set **From** to `0`–`1023` and **To** to `0`–`255`. Connect Potentiometer `value` → Map `value`, and Map `to` → LED `value`.

**Narration:**
> "The Map node is the translator. Tell it what comes in — zero to a thousand and twenty-three — and what should come out — zero to two hundred and fifty-five. It scales everything in between. Knob into Map, Map's *to* output into the brightness."

**On-screen text:** `from 0–1023 → to 0–255`

### 3:10 — Fade it

**Screen:** Hardware close-up. Turn the knob slowly, both directions. Hold for six seconds.

**Narration:**
> "Now the whole knob does something, and it does it evenly."

### 3:35 — Why the pin has a tilde

**Screen:** Change the LED node's **Pin** to `7`. The brightness input becomes unconnectable and its hint reads `requires a ~ pin`. Change it back to 9.

**Narration:**
> "One thing will catch you out. Brightness only works on pins marked with a tilde, which can fake an in-between voltage by switching very fast. Move the LED to pin seven and Studio will not let you connect brightness at all. On an Uno the tilde pins are three, five, six, nine, ten and eleven."

**On-screen text:** `PWM pins on an Uno: 3 · 5 · 6 · 9 · 10 · 11`

### 4:05 — Where else Map is used

**Screen:** The working flow.

**Narration:**
> "Whenever two things disagree about their range, Map goes between them. A knob driving a servo: nought to a thousand and twenty-three, into nought to a hundred and eighty degrees. A light sensor driving a volume. A distance sensor driving a colour. Same node, different numbers."
