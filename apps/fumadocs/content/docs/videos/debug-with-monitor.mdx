## Set up before you record

Build a flow that is **deliberately broken before recording starts**: Potentiometer → Map → Servo, where the Map node's **To** range is set to `0`–`0`. Do not mention that this is staged. The viewer should meet it as they would meet their own bug.

## Script

### 0:00 — A flow that does nothing

**Screen:** The broken flow on the canvas, board connected. Turn the knob. The servo does not move. Cut to the hardware to confirm nothing is happening. Return to the canvas.

**Narration:**
> "Three nodes, all connected. The board is connected. The servo will not move, and nothing on screen is marked as wrong. This is the most common state a flow gets stuck in, and there is a method for it."

### 0:25 — Do not start at the hardware

**Screen:** Hardware shot, hands hovering over the servo wires but not touching them.

**Narration:**
> "The instinct is to re-seat the wires. Do not. You can find out whether the hardware is even involved without touching it, in about thirty seconds."

### 0:50 — Monitor at the source

**Screen:** `Cmd/Ctrl + K`, add a **Monitor** node. Connect the Potentiometer's `value` output to it — the existing edge to Map stays. Set the Monitor to `graph`. Turn the knob: the graph responds.

**Narration:**
> "Connect a Monitor node to the first node in the chain. An output feeds as many inputs as you like, so this disturbs nothing. Turn the knob: the graph moves. The sensor, the wiring, and the board are all fine, so the fault is downstream."

**On-screen text:** `one output → many inputs`

### 1:25 — Monitor in the middle

**Screen:** Add a second Monitor, connected to the Map node's `to` output. Turn the knob. The first graph moves; the second is a flat line at zero.

**Narration:**
> "Now connect one in the middle. Same knob, same turn, and the second graph is flat. Values go into the Map node and something useless comes out. Three suspects down to one, without touching a wire."

### 2:05 — The bug

**Screen:** Open the Map node's settings. Zoom to 200 %. The **To** range reads `0` to `0`. Change it to `0`–`180`. The second graph springs to life; cut to hardware and the servo tracks the knob.

**Narration:**
> "There it is. The output range is zero to zero, so every reading maps onto nothing. Set it to zero to a hundred and eighty, which is what a servo takes, and the chain works."

**On-screen text:** `to: 0–0 → 0–180`

### 2:35 — Graph or raw

**Screen:** Switch one Monitor to `raw`. Show the numbers scrolling. Switch back to `graph`.

**Narration:**
> "Two modes. Graph is for anything continuous: you are reading the shape, whether it is noisy, whether it is stuck. Raw prints the values, for text, for booleans, and for any number you need to read exactly."

### 3:00 — The method

**Screen:** Zoom to fit the whole flow with both Monitors attached.

**Narration:**
> "The method, not the bug. Put a Monitor at the start to prove the input is alive. Put one in the middle to halve the search. Keep halving. Two or three probes find most faults. Leave them on the canvas while you work."

## Deliberate failure

The whole video is the failure. The staged bug must be genuinely present before recording; do not introduce it on camera.

## Post-production notes

- Both Monitor graphs must be visible in the same frame at 1:25. Arrange the canvas for that before recording.
- Do not cut between turning the knob and the graph responding — the lack of a cut is what makes the demonstration trustworthy.
