## Set up before you record

- Leave the LDR leads long and untidy. You want real noise.
- Put a Monitor node in graph mode on the canvas from the start. The graph is the subject of this video.
- Measure the room's dark and light readings before recording, and use those numbers in the narration.

## Script

### 0:00 — The problem, on a graph

**Screen:** LDR → Monitor in graph mode, zoomed so the graph fills half the frame. Nothing moves in the room. The trace jitters.

**Narration:**
> "Nothing in this room is changing, and the reading will not sit still. Every analog sensor does this. It is the last digit or two of a measurement that was never that precise. The trouble starts when you make a decision from it."

### 0:40 — Add Smooth

**Screen:** `Cmd/Ctrl + K`, add **Smooth** between the LDR and the Monitor. Set **Type** to `moving average` and **Window size** to `25`. Hold on the graph for five seconds before and after.

**Narration:**
> "Put a Smooth node in the middle. Moving average, window of twenty-five. It reports the average of the last twenty-five readings instead of the newest one. Watch the graph settle."

### 1:40 — The two modes

**Screen:** Change **Window size** to `100`, then to `5`, pausing on each. Then set **Type** to `smooth` with **Attenuation** at `0.995`, then `0.9`.

**Narration:**
> "Moving average is the blunt one. A bigger window is calmer and slower: at a hundred, the graph is glassy, but cover the sensor and it takes a moment to notice. At five it barely helps.
>
> The other mode uses an attenuation between zero and one. Closer to one smooths harder. It reacts more gracefully than a big window, but it never quite arrives at the value. Start with a moving average of twenty-five."

**On-screen text:** `window 25 · window 100 (laggy) · window 5 (still noisy)`

### 2:20 — Add Compare

**Screen:** Add a **Compare** node after Smooth. Set **Compare as** to `number`, **Test** to `less than`, and **Number** to your measured dark threshold. Connect `true` to the LED node's `true`, and `false` to `false`. Cover the sensor. The LED lights.

**Narration:**
> "Now the decision. Compare answers a yes-or-no question about a value. Number, less than, three hundred — because in this room, under three hundred means someone has covered the sensor. True and false out of Compare go into the LED's true and false."

### 3:10 — Failure: chatter at the boundary

**Screen:** Set the Smooth window to `1`, or bypass Smooth. Hold a hand at the boundary so the reading hovers at the threshold. Cut to the hardware. The LED flickers. Hold for six seconds.

**Narration:**
> "Here is what happens without the smoothing. Hold the light at the boundary, and the reading crosses three hundred dozens of times a second, so the LED does too. On a relay you would hear it buzzing. This sends people looking for a hardware fault that is not there."

**On-screen text:** `[reading oscillating 297–304]`

### 3:50 — Fix it

**Screen:** Restore Smooth with a window of `25`. Repeat the hover. The LED holds steady. Then add a **Delay** node between Compare and the LED, with **Forget previous** on.

**Narration:**
> "Smooth first: the average crosses the line far less often than the raw reading. If it still chatters, a Delay node after the decision makes the flow wait, so a blip never reaches the light. Smooth the input, then slow the output. In that order."

### 4:30 — Recap

**Screen:** Zoom to fit: LDR → Smooth → Compare → LED, with the Monitor on the Smooth node.

**Narration:**
> "Four nodes, and a shape you will use constantly. Sense the world. Shape the reading into something you can trust. Decide from it. Express the result. Keep a Monitor on the middle while you build. It is the only way to see which of the four is lying to you."

## Deliberate failure

The chattering boundary at 3:10, recorded live.

## Post-production notes

- Keep the graph filling at least a third of the frame.
- Do not speed up any graph footage. Timing is the point.
- Use the room's real threshold on the day. Do not say 300 if the sensor reads 640.
