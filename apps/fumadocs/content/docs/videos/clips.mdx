A **clip** is a short, silent, looping demonstration: one node doing its one job, or one
setting changing something visible. No narration, no titles, no cuts. A reader who cannot
play it loses nothing, because every clip's caption says in words what the clip shows.

There are 55 clips, about 21 minutes of finished footage. They are grouped by
the rig you build to shoot them, not by where they appear in the docs. Build a rig once,
then shoot everything on it.

## Rules for every clip

- **10 to 30 seconds.** One idea. If it needs two, it is two clips.
- **Silent.** Clips ship muted and loop. Nothing in a clip may depend on sound.
- **No cuts inside a clip.** A cut hides the thing the clip exists to prove.
- **Loop cleanly.** Start and end on the same state.
- **Frame the canvas at 150 %** wherever a handle or a value has to be legible.
- **Show the whole wait.** If a node takes a second, so does the clip.
- **The caption is the clip.** Whatever a clip demonstrates must already be stated in the
  page's own text. A clip shows; it never tells you something the text does not.

Capture settings, cursor treatment, and the personal details to hide are the same as for the
narrated videos. See the [production guide](/docs/videos/production-guide).

## Pages that get no clip

Checked and deliberately left alone, so nobody re-audits them:

| Pages | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Section indexes — `/docs/learn`, `/docs/how-to`, `/docs/troubleshooting`, `/docs/concepts`, `/docs/contributing`, and the six node category indexes | These are lists of links. A clip on a page you pass through is noise. |
| Glossary — `/docs/concepts/glossary` | Definitions. Nothing to watch. |
| Bug reports — `/docs/contributing/report-bug-or-request` | A form and a checklist. Filming it would teach nobody anything. |
| Variables overview — `/docs/microflow-hardware-bridge/variables` | The two pages below it carry the clips that matter. |

## When a clip is recorded

Add its URL as the `src` of the `<Clip />` tag on the page named beside it. Leave the caption
where it is. The caption is the clip's text equivalent, and it carries the meaning for a
reader who cannot play it and for anything reading these docs as text.
