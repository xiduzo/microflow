An RGB LED is three LEDs in one package. The RGB node drives all three, so you set a colour rather than three separate brightnesses.

The LED shows the mixed colour, then dims without changing hue.
</Clip>

It needs three PWM pins — the ones marked `~` on the board — because mixing colours means holding each channel at a partial brightness. On an Arduino Uno that is pins 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, and 11.

## Inputs

- **`red`** — Red channel, `0`–`255`.
- **`green`** — Green channel, `0`–`255`.
- **`blue`** — Blue channel, `0`–`255`.
- **`alpha`** — Overall brightness, `0`–`100`. Scales all three channels at once, so you can dim a colour without changing it.
- **`off`** — Turn all three channels off.

## Outputs

- **`value`** — The colour currently being shown.

## Settings

- **Pins** — One pin per channel. Defaults: red `9`, green `10`, blue `11`. All three must be PWM pins.
- **Common anode** — Set this if your LED is common-anode. Default `false`.

## Wiring

An RGB LED has four legs. The longest is the common one; the other three are the colour channels.

| Leg | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| Red | Pin 9, through a 220 Ω resistor |
| Green | Pin 10, through a 220 Ω resistor |
| Blue | Pin 11, through a 220 Ω resistor |
| Common | GND for a common-cathode LED, 5 V for a common-anode one |

Each channel needs its own resistor. One resistor on the common leg changes the colour as the mix changes, which is why a "white" made that way comes out tinted.

## Tips

- **Colours inverted?** You have a common-anode LED and the **Common anode** setting is off — or the other way round. That is the only symptom, and it is the first thing to check.
- **No colour at all on one channel?** That channel is on a pin without PWM. Move it.
- **Driving it from a sensor?** Put a [Map](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/shape/map) node in front of each channel to get from the sensor's range into `0`–`255`.
