Microflow connects physical hardware — buttons, lights, sensors, motors — and makes it do something. You write no code. You drag blocks onto a canvas and connect them. The project runs on your board while you build it.

<Video title="Meet Microflow" duration="2:30" script="/docs/videos/meet-microflow">
What Microflow is, and four things people build with it.
</Video>

## What you need

- A computer running Windows, macOS, or Linux. Or Chrome, Edge, or Opera, if you prefer to install nothing.
- An **Arduino Uno**, an **Arduino Nano**, or an **ESP32**.
- A USB cable that carries data. Many cables carry power only.
- The components your project needs. The first tutorial needs none.

## What people build

- Press a button, turn on a light.
- Read a light sensor, dim a lamp to match.
- Detect motion, sound a buzzer.
- Feed a real sensor into a Figma prototype.
- Drive a servo, a stepper, an LED strip, an 8×8 matrix, or a MIDI synth.
- Ask a local AI model a question, then use the answer in the flow.
