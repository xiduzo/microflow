---
title: Nothing happens on the board
description: The flow is running and the values are moving, but the hardware sits there.
---

The canvas looks right, values are updating on the nodes, the board is connected — and the physical thing does nothing. Nothing on screen is red, because as far as Studio is concerned nothing is wrong: it is faithfully driving a pin, and the component is somewhere else.

Work down this list. It is ordered by how often each one is the cause.

## 1. The pin number

The most common cause, every time. The number in the node's settings must match the pin the component is physically in.

- Open the node and read the pin number aloud.
- Trace the wire on the board to the header and read that number.
- If they differ, that is your answer.

Studio cannot detect this. A pin with nothing attached behaves exactly like a pin with a broken component on it.

## 2. Two nodes on the same pin

A pin can be in one mode at a time — input, output, PWM, servo. Two nodes pointing at the same pin means one wins and the other silently does nothing, and which one wins can depend on the order you added them.

**Find it:** open every node that names a pin and list the numbers. Duplicates are the conflict. Two LED nodes on pin 13, or a Button and a Sensor on the same pin, are the classics.

**Fix:** move one to a free pin, or delete it if it was a duplicate.

## 3. The wrong kind of pin

Some things need a specific kind of pin:

- **Dimming an LED, an intensity, or a servo** needs PWM — pins `3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11` on an Uno or Nano, marked `~` on the board. On a non-PWM pin, an LED still works but only on and off, and a servo does not work at all.
- **Analog sensors** need an analog input, `A0`–`A5` on an Uno or Nano.

Open **Show code** — the [sketch warnings](/docs/troubleshooting/sketch-warnings) name the node and the pin.

## 4. The wiring

- **Nothing at all from an LED?** Check the long leg goes to the signal pin and the short leg to ground through a resistor. Backwards, it will not light and nothing will break.
- **A button that never registers?** It needs a pull-up or a pull-down, otherwise the pin floats between values. Wire a resistor, or turn on the node's **Pull-up** setting.
- **A motor, a relay, or a strip that browns out the board?** They draw more current than a USB port supplies. Power them separately and join the grounds.
- **Half a breadboard dead?** Some breadboards split their power rails down the middle.

## 5. Reserved pins

- **Pins 0 and 1** on Uno and Nano are the USB serial lines. Driving them from a flow breaks communication with the board.
- **A4 and A5** on Uno and Nano are also the I²C lines. Do not use them as analog inputs if you are using an [I2C Device](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/i2c-device) or the [NFC reader](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/pn532).
- **GPIO 6–11 on an ESP32** drive the chip's own flash memory. Using them stops the board booting.
- **Pin 13** is fine to use, but it also drives the on-board LED, so that LED mirrors whatever you do.

## 6. Prove which half is broken

Split the problem in two, rather than guessing.

- **Is the output side alive?** Delete everything upstream and drive the output node from a [Constant](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/generate/constant) or an [Interval](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/generate/interval). If the hardware responds, your wiring and pin are fine and the fault is upstream in the flow.
- **Is the input side alive?** Hang a [Monitor](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/monitor) directly on the sense node. Values means the sensor, wiring, and board are fine.

See [Debug with the Monitor node](/docs/how-to/debug-with-monitor-node) for the full method.
