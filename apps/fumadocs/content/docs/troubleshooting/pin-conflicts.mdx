---
title: Nothing happens on the board
description: The flow runs and the values move, but the hardware does nothing.
---

The canvas looks right. The values update. The board is connected. The component does nothing, and nothing on screen is marked as wrong — Studio is driving a pin correctly, and the component is somewhere else.

Work down this list. The causes are ordered by how often each one is to blame.

## 1. The pin number

The number in the node's settings must match the pin the component is plugged into.

1. Open the node and read its pin number.
2. Follow the wire to the board and read the pin number there.
3. Change one of them if they differ.

Studio cannot detect this. A pin with nothing attached behaves exactly like a pin with a broken component on it.

## 2. Two nodes on one pin

A pin holds one mode at a time: input, output, PWM, or servo. If two nodes name the same pin, one node wins and the other does nothing. Which node wins can depend on the order you added them.

1. Open every node that names a pin.
2. List the pin numbers.
3. Move or delete a node for each duplicate.

Two LED nodes on pin 13, or a Button and a Sensor on one pin, are the common cases.

## 3. The wrong kind of pin

Some components need a specific kind of pin.

- **Brightness, intensity, and servo angle need PWM.** On an Uno or a Nano, PWM runs on pins 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, and 11, marked `~` on the board. On a pin without PWM, an LED still switches on and off, and a servo does not work at all.
- **Analog sensors need an analog input**, `A0` to `A5` on an Uno or a Nano.

Open **Show code**. The [sketch warnings](/docs/troubleshooting/sketch-warnings) name the node and the pin.

## 4. The wiring

- **An LED does nothing.** Check that the long leg goes to the signal pin, and the short leg to ground through a resistor. A reversed LED does not light and does not break.
- **A button never registers.** The pin floats without a pull-up or a pull-down. Add a resistor, or turn on the node's **Pull-up** setting.
- **A motor, a relay, or an LED strip resets the board.** They draw more current than a USB port supplies. Power them from a separate supply and join the grounds.
- **Half the breadboard is dead.** Some breadboards split their power rails at the middle.

## 5. Reserved pins

- **Pins 0 and 1 on an Uno or a Nano** are the USB serial lines. Driving them breaks communication with the board.
- **Pins A4 and A5 on an Uno or a Nano** are also the I²C lines. Do not use them as analog inputs alongside an [I2C Device](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/i2c-device) or the [NFC reader](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/sense/pn532).
- **GPIO 6 to 11 on an ESP32** drive the chip's flash memory. The board does not boot if you use them.
- **Pin 13** also drives the on-board LED, which mirrors whatever you send.

## 6. Split the problem in two

Test each half separately instead of guessing.

**Test the output:** delete everything upstream and drive the output node from a [Constant](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/generate/constant) or an [Interval](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/generate/interval). If the component responds, the wiring and the pin are correct, and the fault is upstream.

**Test the input:** connect a [Monitor](/docs/microflow-studio/nodes/express/monitor) directly to the sense node. If values appear, the sensor, the wiring, and the board are correct.

See [Debug with the Monitor node](/docs/how-to/debug-with-monitor-node) for the full method.
